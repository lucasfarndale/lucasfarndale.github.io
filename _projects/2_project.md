Multiplex immunofluorescence gives a data-rich view of spatial biology, but segmentation-based analyses discard morphological information and can only test hypotheses chosen in advance.

Our self-supervised framework learns holistic architectural representations directly from the images, combining cellular morphology, marker interactions and microenvironmental context without human labels. Applied to more than 7,000 tissue images from over 1,800 patients across two cancer types, it outperformed conventional segmentation-based prognostic analyses, and surfaced previously unknown patterns, including a Ki67-mediated immune evasion phenotype in lung adenocarcinoma and a regulatory T-cell-associated immunosuppressive environment in colorectal carcinoma.

This work is currently a preprint on bioRxiv; see [publications](/publications/).
